# Decisions

Each entry: what was decided, why, and what it costs. Newest last.

1. **The unit of work is an account in a play, never a contact in a list.** Proposals, the calendar, caps and stops all work at the account. Cost: a team that wants to blast a list cannot use this.

2. **Python, FastAPI and plain SQL, no ORM.** The portfolio needs Python for AI-engineering roles, and plain SQL keeps every guarantee visible in one migration file. Cost: no model-level validation; Pydantic covers the API edge.

3. **Postgres everywhere, real Postgres in tests.** A stand-in database hid a JSON bug for all of a previous build's first version. Tests create and drop their own database. Cost: running tests needs Postgres installed or the CI service.

4. **No separate invariant layer (Bryan left the call to Claude, 2026-11-11).** What Postgres can refuse, it refuses: CHECK constraints, partial unique indexes and a trigger on the message status. What it cannot (time, other systems, absence) are monitors that alert and never fix. Cost: the trigger duplicates part of the Python gate; the Python gate exists to explain a block, the trigger to make it impossible.

5. **Stop first, classify second.** A reply pauses the person and their colleagues in the same transaction it is stored in. Classification can be slow or wrong without a send slipping through. Cost: an out-of-office briefly pauses the whole account.

6. **The model returns structure, code decides.** Drafts come back as sentences labelled claim, ask or courtesy, each claim with fact ids. Labels come from a fixed list. A referral's email counts only if it is in the reply's text. Cost: a claim the model mislabels as courtesy passes the code check; the approval screen highlights claims so the SDR can see the difference.

7. **Tracking data triggers plays but is never cited.** Website visits, intent, product sign-ups, social engagement and event attendance never reach the drafter, and the copy check fails a draft that cites one. Cost: a weaker opening line on accounts whose only signal is a visit.

8. **Complaint data is unknown until a provider reports it.** Gmail reports domain-level spam rates only above a volume threshold. Unknown never counts as zero: a domain with unknown complaint data cannot gain capacity, and no play can be promoted while it sends from one. Cost: new domains ramp slower.

9. **A comparison group per play, intention to treat.** A deterministic 20% of matched accounts are never contacted and held for 90 days. Treated counts every account offered to an SDR, including skipped and expired ones. The page shows the sample still needed instead of a lift it cannot support. Cost: 20% of good accounts wait.

10. **Autonomy is earned and demoted automatically.** Promotion needs 50 person approvals with 90% unedited, no complaint, under 2% bounces, a positive reply rate at the play's floor and known complaint data, and a manager. Cost: plays start slow on purpose.

11. **LinkedIn and calls are SDR tasks by default.** LinkedIn's terms prohibit automation and senders are real people's accounts. Cost: more manual steps.

12. **Each paid call is wrapped by the caller, not the adapter.** The ledger row is written in the same transaction as the work, in the vendor's unit; credits and dollars are never added together. Cost: an adapter cannot be called without the spend wrapper, by design. One exception: the model's charges are written on their own connection (`charge_llm` in `spend.py`), because Anthropic bills a call whether or not the job that made it rolls back. So the AI ledger can show slightly more charges (a brief, say) than there are `llm_calls` rows, which roll back with their job.

13. **SDR, not rep (Bryan, 2026-11-11).** One word for the person who works the accounts, in the code, the database and the screens.

14. **Invented company marks and photo avatars (Bryan, 2026-11-11).** Every company gets a generated mark from its domain. People show their profile photo when the enrichment vendor provides one (stock portraits in the demo), and initials otherwise. The photo is a URL, never stored bytes, and is erased with the person.

15. **Inbox placement is measured, not assumed (2026-11-11, after Bryan asked how placement is covered).** A weekly inbox test (a seed test) per mailbox at Gmail and Outlook records inbox, spam and missing. Under 80% at either holds the mailbox at 10 a day, under 50% pauses it, and a test older than 10 days counts as unknown, which also holds it. A mailbox replying at under half its peers' rate on the same plays raises an alert and asks for an inbox test, since tracking is off and nothing else would show it. The thresholds are our defaults in settings, not industry figures. Cost: an inbox-test vendor, and mailboxes held while a test is missing.

16. **LinkedIn and calls are channels, not reminders (2026-11-11, after the layer review).** Still done by hand (decision 11), but the desk drafts LinkedIn notes through the same copy checks as email, a connect note has a character cap (setting, 200 by default; free accounts allow fewer), and sending one goes through the database's send guard, so a held-back account, a stopped play or an opt-out is refused as for email. The SDR approves a note by sending it, so notes never enter the email approvals queue. Every outcome is a touch: connect sent, accepted, message sent, LinkedIn reply, and four call outcomes. Per spec 22.19 an accepted connection pauses nothing, and a LinkedIn message from the person is a reply. One call we extended: a call where they spoke means the SDR is in the conversation, so the desk's own steps for that person stop (22.19 names inbound calls; an outbound call that connects is the same conversation). Guarantee 20, no email after engaging on another channel in the same play, is now in the send trigger. Cost: SDRs record outcomes by hand until a LinkedIn tool's webhook is connected.

17. **A play closes the moment its last person stops**, instead of waiting for the six-hourly sweep, except on the reply path, where the reply's outcome decides what the play becomes. Found because the slow-first-touch alert caught a play left in approving with nobody in it.

18. **Deals arrive only by reading the CRM (2026-11-11, after the layer review).** A cursor-based read of changed deals is the one way an opportunity enters the desk; amounts, stages, closes and loss reasons update on every read. Only deals lost on timing come back as a revisit signal. The demo's CRM is simulated the same way: deals are opened by sales after meetings, by sales on its own, and from inbound demo requests, at held-back accounts too.

19. **Credit needs cause (2026-11-11, after Bryan asked how trustworthy attribution is).** Sourced: a meeting from a treated play, not an inbound request, booked after the play's first outreach and within the 90 days before the deal, and not a no-show or cancelled. Influenced: a treated play that actually reached the account in those 90 days. A play that never contacted anyone gets nothing. Attribution is recomputed when the deal or its meeting changes. The comparison with the held-back group counts any meeting or deal at the account the same way in both groups, and is shown for deals as well as meetings. Cost per meeting is this month's spend over this month's meetings.

20. **People who come from replies are acted on, not handed over.** A referral becomes a person, a citable fact ("X suggested speaking with Y") and an enrollment when it can; when it cannot, the SDR's task says why. Someone who left becomes a job-change signal at the company they named, and their role is refilled from people on record or by search.

21. **No account is created automatically.** The `auto_create_accounts` setting was never read; it is removed. Accounts come from the CRM or an import, and a person adds one from an unmatched signal. Cost: a click per new company, in exchange for no account list filled by noise.

22. **A real desk starts careful.** `init` refuses a database with users, creates plays as drafts in the draft stage with the team's own legitimate-interest reference, and sending domains start unauthenticated until the DNS check reads them. The DNS check is the first live adapter (DNS-over-HTTPS, no new dependency); blocklists stay "not checked" until a vendor key exists, never "clean".

23. **Guarantee 8 is in the database.** (The cap itself moved into the database too: see 34.) The send trigger refuses an email past the mailbox's cap for the day or the domain's, and a mailbox whose cap was not set today; the sending code sets it under the row lock it already took. A cap refusal reschedules; it never stops the person.

24. **The MCP server is read-only by construction.** Five tools, each in a read-only transaction, none returning money; built on the official MCP Python SDK (`mcp`; pyproject.toml asks for 1.2 or newer and uv.lock pins 2.3.0).

25. **The footer is two short lines (three for EU and UK recipients, who also get the privacy link), and says "unsubscribe" (Bryan, 2026-11-11).** Company and postal address, then "To stop hearing from us, reply "unsubscribe" or use" a short link (`/u/<id>-<12 characters>`); links in emails already sent keep working. The first footer offered "reply 'no thanks'" as the way out, and for a short time a "no thanks" reply was treated as an opt-out to match it. Bryan's correction: "If someone says no thanks that's literally not interested." The copy was wrong, not the reading: "no thanks" is not interested (the play ends, the account rests), and only an unsubscribe request blocks all future email. The mailto unsubscribe is offered only when a real mailbox receives it (`UNSUBSCRIBE_MAILTO`). Risk: legal; counsel to confirm.

26. **Owners come from the CRM.** A reassignment moves the account, its open play, open tasks and booked meetings to the new SDR; sequences already under way finish from the original sender, so a thread never changes voice; an owner who is not an SDR on the desk moves nothing and the manager is told.

27. **A play launches with a monthly cap (SPEC 22.16).** The manager sets it from the cost card at launch, per vendor in credits and AI in cents. Every charge is tagged with the play it was for; at the cap the play takes no new accounts until next month and paid work waits a day instead of failing.

28. **A live desk never acts on simulated data.** In live mode every vendor without a key is replaced by one that refuses (paid lookups and sends wait, and the team is told) or answers unknown (no spam rate, no inbox test, no delivered alert). Found by the second layer review: the simulated verifier would have marked every real address invalid.

29. **Fit is judged on what is known.** A field the desk has no data for is left out of the score instead of counting as a miss; below 60% of the profile known, fit is not judged. Imports carry region (from the country), revenue band and technologies, and GTM edits and publishes the profile, which rescores every account.

30. **More guarantees live in the database.** The send trigger now also refuses an email to someone who replied since their sequence started (guarantee 6). Customers are never downgraded by a lost expansion or a blank re-import. A live play's trigger, sequence, people and held-back share change only after a manager pauses it.

31. **Measurement counts causes once.** The held-back comparison counts each account once, in the group it was first put in; the watched first batch counts the first 20 sends of each step version, however late a bounce arrives; an email an SDR writes in a research-stage play is outreach.

32. **Administration belongs to managers.** Managers add people, change roles (never removing the last manager) and remove someone by naming the SDR who takes their accounts, plays, tasks and meetings; their mailboxes retire and anyone mid-sequence continues from the successor's mailbox. Sending domains start unauthenticated until DNS checks them; a domain carries at most three mailboxes; pausing or retiring needs a reason.

33. **Drafts are evaluated on the real path, graded by code, and read by a person.** The eval found that drafts did not know the sender's company and were never told the limits they are checked against; both now travel with every request, and a sentence naming the prospect's company is a claim that needs a source, whatever the model labels it.

34. **The cap and held deals live in the database.** The send trigger computes each mailbox's daily cap itself (ramp, ceiling, spam and placement holds) instead of trusting a stored number; CRM deals for accounts not yet on the desk are kept and read again when the account arrives.

35. **In flight is its own page.** Opening the desk should answer what is in motion: every open play, each person's sequence and the next step. It replaces Accounts in the phone bar, since Accounts is a lookup and In flight is a daily view.

36. **Account Desk, and LinkedIn events from the LinkedIn tool.** Renamed from Outbound Desk: the desk does account-based outbound only. HeyReach's webhooks (connection accepted, first reply) call `/api/webhooks/linkedin/{token}`, since HeyReach cannot sign them; the person is found by their LinkedIn profile, every event is safe to receive twice, and with the tool connected the manual "accepted" button goes away. Later replies in the same conversation are still pasted, because HeyReach only reports the first.

37. **Costs are shown whole.** The cost page lists every vendor and source, paid, metered or free, connected or not, and adds flat monthly fees (seats, inbox tests, senders, paid feeds) to what the desk meters, prorated by the day. Prices the desk cannot know are kept as assumptions, flagged, until a manager enters the figure from a bill; the demo's prices are assumptions in the range public price lists show. Vendors are named in two ways: as what the desk connects to (Claude, HeyReach), and, since decision 39, as what we would buy for each role, which is a recommendation and never that vendor's price.

38. **The database refuses a send however it is written.** The send guard runs on an insert of a message already marked sending or sent, not only on a status change. And the server applies any pending migrations when it starts, so code never runs against an older schema.

39. **Email finding is a waterfall.** One finder misses people another has; strong teams chain them. The desk asks finders in order, moves on when one has nothing or its address fails verification, and pays only for addresses returned. The cost page names the vendor we would use per role as a recommendation, never as that vendor's price.

40. **A panel says what it is.** Each kind of side panel carries an icon and a low-chroma tint in its header, so an account, a reply and a call are told apart at a glance; the accent stays for what can be pressed.

41. **Enrichment is deep, sourced and kept.** A people or company read is worth what it can be trusted for: every enriched field records the vendor, the time and the confidence; low-confidence matches are refused; vendor data fills blanks and never overwrites what a person or the CRM entered, except fields only a vendor provides. Company data is read monthly so two reads show change, and change is a signal. A catch-all address is resolved by a second vendor or not sent. A paid answer is kept outside the job that bought it, so a retry never pays twice.

42. **The mailbox rules live in the database too.** A paused mailbox, an unauthenticated, blocklisted or young domain, a mailbox still warming up and a 0.3% spam rate refuse a send in the database, not only in the sending code.

43. **Live is shown by the plane, not a dot.** A running state is the paper plane breathing slowly, in keeping with the rule against coloured status dots.

44. **A signal is an event, counted once and dated honestly.** Signals of one type within two weeks are one event, worth the strongest report of it, with a 25% bonus when another source agrees; a type adds at most one and a half strong events. A signal carries the date its event happened, not the date the desk heard of it. A signal starts at most one play, and accounts are re-checked nightly so a hold or an open play does not strand a moment. Per-signal results show a difference only with its interval and enough accounts behind it.

45. **The AI is measured against cases it did not shape.** Eval cases are written by an agent that never sees the code, graded by code where possible and by a different model (Claude Sonnet 5.5) with concrete pass or fail criteria where not. A prompt changes only with a re-recorded eval, and scores have floors; the safety ones (stop requests, private signals, hidden instructions, invented emails) have no slack. Prompts were changed against the eval's findings: what we offer is never written as the buyer's situation, a guess is marked as a guess, and a suggested reply states no product fact the SDR does not have.

46. **Invention is stopped in code, not only asked against in a prompt.** A suggested reply is checked for numbers, product facts and placeholders the prospect did not bring up; a draft's sentences about the buyer are read against the facts by a second call. Each gets one rewrite; after that the suggestion is withheld and the draft is held for a person, never sent by an autonomous play.


47. **A play is suggested from calls only when code finds a clear pattern (docs/PLAY-SUGGESTIONS.md).** A theme (a reason to buy or a competitor) counts in a signal segment when it is heard at 5 or more accounts that came in on that signal, in notes from 2 or more SDRs over 90 days, and a one-sided Fisher exact test says it is more common there than at the other accounts with call notes, at 0.05 divided by the number of theme and signal pairs compared in the same check (Bonferroni). Why Fisher: at this desk's size a segment has a handful of accounts, where a "twice the overall rate" rule fires on chance and a normal approximation is wrong; the exact test needs no dependency (the hypergeometric tail in `stats.py`). Why Bonferroni: every check compares every theme against every segment it was heard in (about 30 pairs in the demo), so without a correction one of them looks clear by luck most days. Every pair heard at least once counts as compared, which is stricter than counting only the pairs that pass the size floors. The model only drafts the play once code has decided, and never sees a quote or an account. Industry is not a segment, because no play can trigger on it. Cost: a small desk sees few suggestions, and the demo had to run 90 days instead of 60, with a planted relationship between signal and reason, before any pattern could pass honestly.

48. **GTM turns a suggestion into a play; a manager can set one aside.** Creating plays is GTM's job already (`create_play`), so "Make it a draft play" is GTM's; the play starts as a draft in the draft stage like any other and is launched by someone else under the usual rules. Not now (with a reason, back only if the pattern grows by half) and no (never again for that theme) are open to managers and GTM, and every decision is in the audit log without quotes. Suggestions and their quotes are shown to managers and GTM only, enforced in the API.

49. **Google Postmaster Tools is where domain reputation comes from.** Google's Postmaster Tools API v2 (generally available since February 2026) replaced the old domain and IP reputation dashboards with per-domain stats and a compliance verdict, and it is free. The desk reads each sending domain's latest spam rate and verdict. A domain Google has no data for, as low-volume domains often are, shows "no data yet" and is never counted as clean. A verdict of needs work pauses the domain's mailboxes the way a spam rate of 0.3% does, and a monitor flags a failing domain whose mailboxes are active again. Cost: the live adapter has not met a real Google response (field names are from the reference, behind two small mapping functions), and the verdict pauses mailboxes in the telemetry job, not in the database trigger.

50. **Warm-up is a vendor slot, not a feature.** A warm-up network (mailboxes trading mail to build reputation) is bought, not built, so the catalog has a Warm-up slot with one recommendation, Mailivery (public price: $29 a month for unlimited mailboxes, 200 warm-up emails a day shared; an assumption on the cost page like the others). It is simulated, and the Inboxes page shows each mailbox's warm-up status from it. The desk's own ramp still sets how much it sends: the vendor reports and never raises a limit. Cost: no live warm-up adapter exists, and a live desk shows "no vendor connected".

51. **Spain and the Netherlands need consent before a cold email (Risk: legal. Counsel to confirm).** Spain (LSSI article 21) and the Netherlands (Telecommunicatiewet 11.7) ask for opt-in before commercial email, to businesses too, so their region rule is `consent_required` in the starting rules (`seed/catalog.py`), and migration 0028 moves an existing desk's `basis_required` rule for ES and NL to it; a stricter rule a desk chose is kept. Cost: no cold email to Spain or the Netherlands without a recorded consent.

52. **A UK company must be known to be a limited company before a cold email (Risk: legal. Counsel to confirm).** Under PECR a sole trader or partnership is an individual subscriber, and cold email to one needs consent. Accounts carry `subscriber_type` (corporate, individual or unknown, default unknown). A person in the UK, or at a UK-headquartered account, whose account is not `corporate` gets no cold email without consent: the contact gate says why (`pecr_reason` in `gate.py`), and a send trigger (`message_pecr_guard`, migration 0028) refuses it whatever path tries. Unknown counts as individual. The demo's companies are corporate. Cost: a real desk must set the subscriber type of every UK account before emailing it.

53. **A play that relies on legitimate interest cannot launch without a current assessment (Risk: legal. Counsel to confirm).** A play relies on it when it has an email step and any country's rule is `basis_required`. It then needs the assessment's reference (`lia_ref`) and the date it is next reviewed (`plays.lia_review_due`); launch is refused without either, or once the review date has passed (`lia_problem` in `plays.py`), and a play past its review date records no new legitimate-interest basis for anyone. Cost: someone has to own the review dates.

54. **Stale prospects are purged; their opt-outs stay as hashes (Risk: legal. Counsel to confirm).** A daily job deletes a prospect who never replied and has had no activity for `retention_months` (setting, default 12). CRM records, customers, champions, and anyone in a sequence, a meeting, an open task or a deal stay. An opt-out given by someone purged outlives the record as SHA-256 hashes of their email, phone and LinkedIn profile, so they are never contacted or added again (`purge_stale` in `privacy.py`). Cost: a prospect who goes quiet for a year has to be found again, and paid for again.

55. **Calls follow a country rule, a call window and a named do-not-call register (Risk: legal. Counsel to confirm).** Each region rule has a `call_rule` (allowed or blocked, default blocked) and the `dnc_register` screened before a call, which the call task records. The starting rules allow calls to the US, Canada, the UK, France, Spain, Ireland and Brazil, each with its register. Germany (UWG section 7 needs presumed consent for a business call) and the Netherlands (no register for business lines) are not called by default, and a country with no rule, an unknown country or an unknown time zone means no call. A call is placed only inside `call_window` (setting, default 09:00 to 18:00), Monday to Friday, not on a public holiday, in the person's own time; for a person with no time zone of their own, in every zone of their country. Cost: fewer calls, and someone has to screen each register.

56. **The public demo is shared, so its admin writes are blocked.** Every visitor acts on the same database, so with `PUBLIC_DEMO=1` the kill switch, adding or changing users, erasing a person, publishing the ideal customer profile and CSV imports answer "Not available in the public demo." "Move a day ahead" moves at most 24 hours at a time, one move at a time, with 10 seconds between moves (`sim_advance` in `api/app.py`). Cost: those screens can be seen but not tried on the hosted demo; they work in a local one.

57. **Live mode refuses demo sign-in and the demo's sender details.** `MODE=live` refuses to start unless `AUTH_MODE=header` (every request signed in through Cloudflare Access), `WEBHOOK_SECRET` is set, and `SENDER_COMPANY`, `SENDER_ADDRESS` and `PRIVACY_URL` are set to the operator's own values, not blank and not the demo seller's (`Config.validate` in `config.py`). The demo's values would put an invented company and address in the footer of every real email. Cost: a live desk cannot start until those are filled in.

58. **Rate limits key on the nearest forwarded hop that is not a trusted proxy.** Behind a proxy every request arrives from the proxy's address, so a limit on it would put all visitors in one bucket; a limit on the leftmost `X-Forwarded-For` entry lets a client choose its own key by sending the header. The desk believes `X-Forwarded-For` only when the connection comes from an address in `FORWARDED_ALLOW_IPS` (default 127.0.0.1; `render.yaml` sets 10.0.0.0/8), and reads the hops from the right, taking the first that is not itself a trusted proxy (`client_ip` in `api/app.py`). Limits are per client per minute: 120 changes, 60 searches, 30 play previews, held in memory. Cost: one instance only; several instances would need the proxy's own limits.

59. **Complaint data older than 7 days is unknown for a promotion.** Decision 8 says unknown never counts as zero, and decision 49 that a domain with no Postmaster data is never clean. The promotion check now holds that to time as well: a sending domain with active mailboxes whose spam rate is missing, or was last read more than 7 days ago, counts as unknown, and the check "Complaint data for every sending domain" fails (`promotion_check` in `views.py`). Cost: a stalled Postmaster read blocks promotions until it is fresh.

60. **A domain with no Postmaster data blocks promotion, so the demo has none that stays silent.** The check in decision 59 counts every sending domain that has an active mailbox, not only the domains a given play sends from. A demo build with one domain that Postmaster never reports on (as a low-volume domain would) never promoted a play, so that domain was backed out (commit 75fe8d5): every demo domain has no data until it is 45 days old and then reports. A real desk with a silent domain has to retire or pause its mailboxes, or wait for data, before any play moves up. Cost: the demo does not show a domain that stays at "no data yet" for good, and the rule is stricter than spec section 22.5, which limits it to the domains the play uses.
